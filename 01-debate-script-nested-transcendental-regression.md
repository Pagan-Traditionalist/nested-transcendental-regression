# Question-First Debate Script: The Nested Transcendental Regression

**Debate strategy:** Know the transcendental answer in advance, but proceed question-first. Each answer is the given for the next question. The chain moves from an objective representation, to the subject–object structure of that representation, to the subject-pole of that act, to the empirical ego as appearing content, to conscious presence that does not wait on a further objectifying act.

> Owner lock: use **sword** throughout (not knife). Ideal line for the sword diagnostic stops at steps 1–2; step 3 only if interlocutor identifies the subject with the ordinary empirical me; step 4 kept off this line unless the topic has shifted.

---

## Step 1 — Objective Representation

Start with:

> **If the sword is shiny, then the sword is reflective.**  
> **The sword is shiny.**  
> **Therefore, the sword is reflective.**

Then stop:

> **Question:** What are the necessary conditions for P2 — “the sword is shiny” — to be an objective representation of empirical reality at all?

If they fight the inference, let them. That is not the fight. The syllogism can be valid and still not answer the transcendental question.

If they answer with another belief, proposition, evidence, or justification:

> **Follow-up:** I’m not asking what proposition justifies it. I’m asking what must already be the case for there to be an empirical sword represented as shiny.

Do not try to defeat “why not a brain?” here. Step 1 only has to lock the subject–object correlation.

### Your Answer

There must be **Subject ↔ Object**, and the empirical object must be represented under:

- **Space** — somewhere, extended, here/there, distinct from other objects.
- **Time** — now/before/after, persistence and change.
- **Causality** — sensory affection is referred to an empirical object as its cause, so there is an object rather than merely an unordered sensation.

### Conclusion

> **Objective empirical representation requires a subject representing an object under space, time, and causality.**

> **Given that we have that subject–object structure, what must be true of whatever functions as subject in that act?**

---

## Step 2 — The Subject Qua Subject

> **Question:** Given the required subject–object structure, what must be true of the subject-pole such that the object appears *to* it, rather than the subject-pole itself appearing as an object in the same act?

### Your Answer

In that particular act, the subject must:

- **be that for whom the object appears**, and
- **remain the nonobjectified pole of that act.**

The phrase **of that particular act** is the whole point. This is a distinction of roles, not yet a claim that there are two numerically different entities.

Then ask:

> **Can the subject functioning as subject simply be another object in that same experience?**

If they say reflection objectifies the self: reflection proves the reflected empirical self is now object, while a subject-pole is still presupposed. Reflection moves the subject–object boundary; it never abolishes it.

### Conclusion

> **Whatever occupies the subject-role in a given act is not, in that same respect, the object of that act. The moment it is made an object, another subject-pole is already presupposed.**

> **Given that, is this subject-pole simply the ordinary empirical “me”?**

---

## Step 3 — The Empirical Ego *(branch / frontier — not required on the ideal sword line)*

> **Question:** Given that the subject-pole is not the object qua object in that act, what must be true for the empirical “me” itself to appear as an object of experience?

Body, memories, desires, emotions, personality, biography, willing can appear as objects. Their appearing presupposes a subject-pole for whom they appear.

> **The empirical ego, as appearing content, cannot exhaust the subject-pole that is the condition of its appearing.**

Do **not** overclaim numerical nonidentity. Hold: the brain/body/empirical person *as appearing object* is not the subject-pole *of the act in which they appear*.

Schopenhauer’s aesthetic case may illustrate after the argument; it does not establish Pure Subject of Knowing as a necessary condition inside this step.

### Conclusion

> **The empirical ego as empirically appearing and determinable does not exhaust the subject-pole presupposed in its appearing.**

> **Given that both the empirical object and the empirical ego can be given within consciousness, what must be true of that consciousness for this givenness to be consciously present?**

---

## Step 4 — Nonobjectifying Presence *(off the ideal sword line unless topic shifted)*

> **Question:** If both empirical objects and the empirical ego can be given within consciousness, what must be true for that consciousness itself to be present without requiring endless further objectification?

If presence of C₁ requires a further conscious objectifying act C₂, and so on, how is any of it present at all?

At least some conscious presence cannot wait on a further objectifying act (**prereflective self-manifestness** in that limited sense). Unconscious higher-order stories stop the regress but owe givenness. Intrinsic first-order consciousness grants the anti-regress result under another label.

Do not force a unique metaphysical picture (self-luminous field) over rival labels. Force the negative: **objectifying mediation cannot be the ground of conscious presence.**

### Conclusion

> **Consciousness cannot first acquire presence by becoming an object for another consciousness. At least some presence must be nonobjectifying and prereflective.**

---

## The Four Questions to Memorize

1. What must already be the case for “the sword is shiny” to be an objective empirical representation at all? → Subject ↔ Object under Space, Time, Causality.
2. Given that structure, what must be true of whatever functions as subject in that act? → Nonobjectified pole of that particular act.
3. Given that, what must be true for the empirical “me” itself to appear as an object? → A subject-pole for whom it appears; appearing content does not exhaust that pole.
4. If both can be given within consciousness, what must be true for that consciousness to be present? → At least some presence does not wait on a further objectifying act.

**Governing logic:** each answer supplies the necessary condition presupposed by the next question. Use **Given that …** rather than a mere **Then**.
